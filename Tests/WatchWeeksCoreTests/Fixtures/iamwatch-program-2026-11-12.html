The Precision Olympics: The Observatory Competitions and the Making of Horological Prestige

Wonder Room

Between the 1860s and the 1960s, Swiss watchmaking settled its claims to precision in public. Each year a few hundred chronometers were deposited at an observatory, observed for weeks against the stars, and ranked to the hundredth of a second in a printed table. Neuchâtel, Geneva, Kew, Hamburg and Besançon each ran its own contest under its own rules, so a first prize in one place meant something quite different in another. This lecture follows the competitions from the first règlements to the last bulletins de marche, and shows how a column of figures became the hardest currency of reputation the industry has ever had.

Presenter Carlos Torres (Founder, The Ministry of Horological Affairs)

Standing on Tradition – The Historical Development of Innovation in Watchmaking

Wonder Room

Tradition in watchmaking has never stood still. Andreas Strehler examines how established methods have been questioned, adapted and developed over time, and how new ideas emerge from knowledge passed between generations. Looking at the historical development of innovation in watchmaking, the lecture considers the relationship between tradition and progress: when established methods should be preserved, when they should be challenged, and how changing them can become part of carrying the craft forward.

Régleurs: Watchmaking's Invisible Masters of Precision

Wonder Room

The names on the dials of the winning chronometers are seldom the names of the people who made them run. Behind them stood the régleurs, the specialists who poised the balance, shaped the hairspring and brought a movement to within a tenth of a second a day. Some worked for a single house. Others regulated for eleven rival firms in the same year, which the published tables reveal only to a reader who knows how to look. Drawing on competition registers read piece by piece, this lecture reconstructs careers such as those of Otto Kaurup, Borgstedt and Paul Perret, and asks why an industry so dependent on these men kept them anonymous.

Presenter Carlos Torres (Founder, The Ministry of Horological Affairs)

Who Actually Invents Anything Anymore?

Wonder Room

Every maker claims innovation. Genuine invention, a mechanism or idea that did not exist before, is rarer than the marketing suggests. Kari Voutilainen, Roger W. Smith, Andreas Strehler and Rexhep Rexhepi ask whether independent artisanal watchmaking is still capable of producing genuinely new ideas, or whether much of what we call innovation is refinement of what came before. And the uncomfortable follow-up: if something truly new did emerge, would collectors recognise or value the difference?

Life & Times of George Daniels

Wonder Room

Roger W. Smith explores the life, ideas and working methods of George Daniels through the watches he made. 18th & 19th Century French Precision Horology

Wonder Room

The Co-Axial – From Idea to Reality

Wonder Room

Roger W. Smith traces the story of George Daniels’ co-axial escapement and its long journey from an experimental idea to a system capable of series production. Roger worked alongside George during a crucial period in its development, giving him a direct perspective on the challenges involved in turning an invention into something that could be produced at scale. The lecture follows that evolution, and the persistence and development required to take the co-axial from idea to reality.

Presenter Roger W. Smith (Founder and Watchmaker, Roger W. Smith Limited)

Does Independence Need the Groups?

Wonder Room

The big houses train the watchmakers, sustain the suppliers and set the prices every independent is measured against. The independents give the industry its new ideas and much of its moral authority. Three leaders of major maisons and one man who walked out of a group to go alone ask whether the two worlds are rivals, patrons of each other, or a single ecosystem that prefers not to admit it. The closing argument for a simple idea: nobody in this industry is independent alone.
